Efficient and robust approximate nearest neighbor search using Hierarchical Navigable Small World graphs

Yu. A. Malkov, D. A. Yashunin
Abstract We present a new approach for the approximate nearest neighbor search problem based on a class of randomized graphs called Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs. The proposed model uses a multi-layer structure where each layer represents a subset of the graph nodes, mimicking a skip-list.

By routing the search queries dynamically through coarser graphs at the top layers down to dense graphs at the base layer, HNSW achieves logarithmic complexity scaling O(log N) on search times.

Our evaluations demonstrate that HNSW outperforms previous state-of-the-art vector indexing methods (like kd-trees or product quantization) by trade-off speed for accuracy, providing sub-millisecond retrieval on million-scale datasets.

The search algorithm is fully autonomous, robust, and highly parallelizable, making it the industry standard for modern vector databases.
